Swing Back (Center Back)

← Beginner's Guide

The swing back (also called the center back or fullback) controls the middle of the formation. You are primarily a defender, but you can attack forward when you see the opportunity. See Backs in the 3-3 for how the swing fits into the Seven formation and when to rotate in for the strong-side back.

Your partners are the two wing backs — you must always be ready to support them, and you can also support the forwards and pivot as needed.

On offense

When you have the puck:

  • Your first thought should be to pass to the forwards or pivot.
  • If you pass to the pivot, stay down until they pass or drive.
  • Throw the puck as early as safely possible.
  • Swim straight ahead or toward either side of the formation, but stay in the middle — do not cross the formation.
  • If you cannot pass, swim to create a passing option.
  • Do not curl.
  • All of your help is in front of you.
  • If you are in trouble, send the puck to the inside forward.
  • If you are on the wall, send the puck forward and get off the wall.
  • You may pass to the wing backs, but stay down and support until they are in the clear.

When you no longer have the puck:

  • Surface backwards (unless you are on the attack and following the play).

When a teammate has the puck:

  • When a forward or pivot has the puck behind you, surface backwards.
  • When a wing back has the puck behind you, surface backwards.
  • When a forward has the puck in front of you, trail the play in the middle of the formation, slightly behind the back that is supporting the play. Expect the puck at any time.
  • When the pivot has the puck, trail the play in the middle — ideally 5–6 feet back of the puck. If the pivot charges forward, treat the play like an arrowhead.

On defense

  • Control the middle of the pool and the opponent’s forward line.
  • Allow the wing forwards and wing backs to control the wall and funnel the puck to you.
  • No one swims down the middle without you hitting them.
  • Force the other team to go lateral into your players. Do not allow them to go straight down the pool.
  • When the puck is wall side, cover the inside back if they are beaten.
  • When the puck is openside, cover the outside back and push the puck toward the wall to stop the attack.
  • You are often the second defender and the last back, because the wing backs play aggressively.
  • Support the wing backs as they push the play inside. When possible, take the puck and go on the attack.
  • If the play comes to you and you cannot control it, force it laterally across the formation to the other back. Do not give ground.
  • Attack from the middle, not from the edges — unless the puck is directly in front of your goal (then all backs attack the puck to the corner).
  • Don’t collapse all the way to the wall — you’ll be in the way of the wing forward or wing back. But if a wing back gets beaten, covering deep is acceptable.
  • It’s expected that both you and a wing back will be down at the same time. Trust your wing backs — you do not have to be first into every scrum.
  • When the puck is behind you (closer to the goal you’re defending), surface backwards toward the center of the goal. Do not backpick — that is the forward’s job.
