Wing Back (Outside Back)

← Beginner's Guide

The wing back (also called an outside back or halfback) controls the outside of the formation on the left or right. The back closest to the wall at any moment is the inside back; the one furthest from the wall is the openside back. These labels swap as the puck moves — see Backs in the 3-3 for how the Seven formation lines up.

In the Seven formation, the strong-side back (the back on the puck side) is usually on the bottom. The terminology differs, but the job is the same: control your side and don’t get beat to the wall.

Your partner is the wing forward on your side.

On offense

When you have the puck:

  • Your first thought should be to pass to your forward or the pivot.
  • Throw the puck as early as safely possible.
  • Swim straight ahead or toward your side of the formation. Do not cross the formation.
  • Do not curl with the puck.
  • All of your help is in front of you or toward the middle of the formation.
  • If you start to lose the puck, force it toward the pivot.
  • If you are on the wall on your side, swim forward up the wall or pass off the wall.
  • Do not pass to the swing back.

When you no longer have the puck:

  • Surface backwards.

When a teammate has the puck:

  • When your wing forward has the puck behind you, surface backwards.
  • When your wing forward has the puck in front of you, trail directly behind them. Expect the puck to be dropped back to you at any time.
  • When the pivot has the puck, be ready to receive a pass on your side — ideally 5–6 feet away and 5–6 feet back of the puck.
  • When the swing has the puck and you are the inside back, be ready to receive a pass about 5–6 feet away and 1–2 feet in front of the puck.
  • When the swing has the puck and you are the openside back, be prepared to support a pass going to the forward in front of you.

When the puck is in your wall channel:

  • Play up close to the wall.
  • If the puck comes back to you from the forwards, you can pass it back up to the forward, swim with it up the wall if the forward surfaces, or go forward at 45 degrees with a pass to the pivot. If you pass to the pivot, chase the play until it clears to the other wing.

On defense

  • Control the outside of the formation. The other team cannot beat you wide.
  • Do not let the play go down your wall.
  • Funnel opposing forwards toward the pivot.
  • Attack from the outside in, unless the puck is directly in front of your goal (in that case, all backs attack the puck to the corner — positions don’t matter).
  • Once the puck is lost in front of you, chase the play from your side.
  • If you cannot control the play, force it to the pivot or the wall and surface backwards quickly.
  • If you are the openside back and the swing forces the play to your side, attack aggressively — you are now the inside back.
